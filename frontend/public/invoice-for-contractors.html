What every contractor invoice needs in Canada

A Canadian contractor invoice is more than a price tag taped to a quote. The CRA, your client's bookkeeper, and your own records all need the same information laid out clearly. Miss any of it and you spend a week chasing an email chain instead of cashing a cheque.

  • Business name and address. Whatever name your bank account is in. Sole proprietorships use the owner's legal name; incorporated businesses use the registered name with Inc., Ltd., or Corp.
  • Client name and address. The exact billing entity. If you billed Acme Renovations and the cheque needs to come from Acme Holdings, write Acme Holdings.
  • Invoice number. Sequential, never reused. The CRA expects an audit trail. InvoicePrepper auto-numbers every invoice per user.
  • Invoice date. The day you issued it, not the day work happened.
  • Itemized line items. Labour separated from materials, each with a description, quantity, unit price, and line total. Lump sums get challenged.
  • Subtotal, tax, and total. Tax shown as its own line. If you are registered for GST or HST your number goes on every invoice.
  • Payment terms. Net 14 or net 30. Without them, clients default to "whenever."

Three things contractors get wrong on invoices

These three invoicing mistakes are the most common reasons contractors get paid late. All three are avoidable.

1. Lump-sum line items "Bathroom reno: $4,800" tells your client nothing. They cannot verify it, their bookkeeper cannot file it, and you cannot defend it if a dispute lands. Break out labour hours, fixtures, plumbing supplies, drywall, paint, and disposal as separate lines. Same total, far fewer questions.
2. Missing GST or HST number If you are registered for GST or HST and the number is not on the invoice, your business client cannot claim the input tax credit. They will withhold payment, ask for a corrected invoice, and you wait another two weeks. InvoicePrepper has a tax number field in your profile that auto-prints on every invoice.
3. No payment terms An invoice without terms is a suggestion. Net 14 or net 30 puts a deadline in writing. Late fees of 1.5 percent per month after the due date are standard and legal in Canada if disclosed up front. Add the line "Net 14. 1.5% per month on overdue balances." to your default invoice notes and watch how much faster cheques arrive.

How contractors actually get paid faster

Most "get paid faster" advice is either generic ("send invoices on time") or unrealistic ("require deposits from every client"). What actually moves the needle for working contractors is shorter feedback loops between finishing a job and the client receiving an invoice they can act on.

Same-day invoicing. Industry data is consistent: invoices sent the day a job ends get paid roughly 30 percent faster than invoices sent a week later. The reason is simple. The work is fresh in the client's mind, the receipt of services is unambiguous, and they do not need to dig back through their calendar to verify what you did.

Deposit on jobs over a thousand dollars. A 30 to 50 percent deposit covers your materials, locks the client's commitment, and means the final invoice is half the size and half as scary to approve. Contractors who require deposits report fewer cancellations and faster final-payment cycles.

e-Transfer over cheque. A cheque takes 5 to 10 business days from the client's desk to your bank account. e-Transfers clear the same day. Add your e-Transfer email or autodeposit address directly to the invoice notes field and most clients will use it.
